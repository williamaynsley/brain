# CPC and CPM are the same number twice — you rarely choose between them
date: 2026-09-01

The identity: CPM = CPC x CTR x 1000. They are not rival pricing options, they are two views of one spend, joined by click-through rate. Fix any two, the third is determined. So 'should I buy CPC or CPM' is mostly a false choice.

Verified on L'ange Google, 30d Aug 2026 (Marketer.com platform):
- Brand Search US:  CPC $0.5457 x 30.56% x 1000 = $166.77 = reported CPM $166.77
- NB Hot Brushes:   CPC $1.9629 x 22.14% x 1000 = $434.69 = reported CPM $434.69
- PMax Hero:        CPC $1.7191 x  1.32% x 1000 = $22.64  = reported CPM $22.64
Three for three, exact.

WHAT YOU ACTUALLY CHOOSE is the optimization goal / bid strategy (what event you are asking the platform to buy) and the TARGET (tROAS/tCPA level). The billing model is then handed to you by platform + campaign type: Google Search and Shopping bill per click and you cannot buy search impressions; Google Display/Video/Demand Gen can bill per impression or per view; Meta bills per impression for essentially every objective, and the choosable part is the optimization goal, not the billing event.

WHAT CPM AND CPC ARE FOR: diagnosis, not selection.
- CPM moves with auction competition, audience narrowness, and creative appeal at the scroll.
- CPC = CPM / CTR / 1000, so CPC rising while CPM stays flat means CTR is falling = creative fatigue.
- NEVER compare CPM across campaign types. L'ange PMax at $22.64 vs search at $434.69 is a 19x gap that says nothing about quality — one buys cheap Display/YouTube volume, the other buys a handful of very high-intent search impressions.
